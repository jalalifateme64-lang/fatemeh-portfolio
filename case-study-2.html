Case study 02 · Bundling app

Rebuilding a declining bundle feature through a scalable template system

Volume Discount's creation rate had dropped from ~37% to ~12% over seven months. I led research across a merchant-journey audit, screen recordings, competitor benchmarking, and internal team input — then designed a templating system that solved both a UX problem and a technical one.

Role
Lead product designer
Timeline
~7 months
Team
PM, 2 engineers, data analyst
Methods
Merchant research, benchmarking
The problem

A steady decline, with no obvious cause

Volume Discount is a bundling feature that lets merchants set up tiered, quantity-based discounts. Over seven months, its creation rate — the share of merchants who started setup and actually published a discount — fell from roughly 37% to 12%. No single change explained the drop, which meant the problem was systemic rather than a broken step in the flow.

My job was to find out why merchants were dropping off, and whether the fix was a UX problem, a technical one, or both.

My role

Owning research through delivery

I led the investigation end to end: framing the research questions, running the studies, and turning findings into a design a small engineering team could actually ship without breaking existing merchants' bundles.

  • Designed and ran the merchant-journey audit and screen-recording review
  • Ran competitor benchmarking across comparable bundling apps
  • Synthesized findings with the PM and engineers into design requirements
  • Designed the templating system and validated it before rollout
Process

Four angles on the same drop-off

Triangulating research methods to separate the UX problem from the technical one
  1. 01 — Merchant-journey audit

    Mapping every step from intent to publish

    Reconstructed the full creation flow, step by step, to find where merchants were most likely to abandon setup.

  2. 02 — Screen recordings

    Watching where people actually got stuck

    Reviewed recorded sessions of merchants attempting setup, which surfaced confusion around abstract bundle-type names that didn't map to merchants' mental models.

  3. 03 — Competitor benchmarking

    Checking how others frame the same decision

    Compared how competing bundling apps present discount types, to see whether the abstraction problem was specific to us or an industry-wide pattern worth solving differently.

  4. 04 — Internal team input

    Surfacing the technical constraint early

    Worked with engineering to understand backward-compatibility requirements for merchants already running live bundles — a constraint any redesign would have to respect.

Key decisions

Two problems, one system

Decision 01

Replace abstract bundle types with a template system

Challenge

Merchants couldn't tell what a bundle type would look like to their customers until after building it — the low visibility was driving drop-off.

Decision

Designed a set of pre-built, visually previewed templates so merchants could recognize the right bundle type before committing to setup.

Decision 02

Keep existing bundles compatible by default

Challenge

Any redesign risked breaking bundles merchants already had live in their stores.

Decision

Mapped every existing configuration onto the new template system automatically, so no merchant needed to rebuild a bundle that already worked.

Result

Creation rate recovered — and kept more trials

37% Before the decline
12% At its lowest point
→
27% After the template system shipped

"The redesign didn't just fix a UX problem — it removed a technical blocker that had been limiting the feature all along."

Trial cancellation also dropped 4%, suggesting the fix reached beyond the immediate feature.
